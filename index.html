Artifact 1 · Assignment 1.4 · AI Lab

CyberSafe AI: Your Friendly Online Safety Assistant

A beginner-friendly cybersecurity education chatbot prototype developed in Mizou.

Introduction

CyberSafe AI is a chatbot prototype intended to help everyday users, students, and employees recognize common online safety risks and understand safer actions. It applies an AI conversational interface to a practical cybersecurity education use case.

Objective

Explore how a conversational AI assistant can explain phishing, suspicious messages and links, and password-reuse risks in clear language for users who may not have a technical security background.

Process

  • Explored LLM capabilities, Custom GPT concepts, and STORM AI research as part of the project work.
  • Applied design-thinking considerations to shape a beginner-friendly assistant.
  • Built the chatbot prototype in Mizou.
  • Tested three scenarios: a suspicious bank SMS, a suspicious bank login URL, and password reuse.
  • Reviewed the responses and documented limitations, including incorrect repeated personalization and the absence of live URL validation or malware scanning.

Tools and technologies

Mizou chatbot builder, large language model (LLM) experimentation, Custom GPT exploration, and STORM AI research. The project also involved scenario-based prototype testing.

Value proposition of the artifact

Unique value: This project turns AI concepts into a practical, user-facing cybersecurity education prototype and includes testing rather than presenting the chatbot as automatically reliable.

Relevance: It is relevant to organizations exploring AI-assisted security awareness, user education, and conversational support. The documented limitations also demonstrate the importance of human verification and clear boundaries for AI tools.

References and supporting material

The original project report contains the project documentation and supporting information. The working prototype is linked separately.

An accessible explanation of neural network structure, learning, and real-world applications.

This discussion explains neural networks through the example of learning to recognize a cat. It introduces the way layers of connected units process information and how a network adjusts its predictions as it learns.

Objective

Describe the basic structure and learning process of a neural network, including input, hidden, and output layers, weights, prediction errors, and backpropagation.

Process

  • Used a familiar image-recognition example to introduce the topic.

  • Connected neural network concepts to applications in healthcare, cybersecurity, self-driving cars, and virtual assistants.

Tools and technologies

The artifact focuses on conceptual explanation rather than a coded model implementation. The methods discussed include neural network layers, weights, backpropagation, and optimization. No specific software framework is identified in the discussion submission.

Artifact 4 · Assignment 4.3

Data Challenge Scenarios

Analysis of data quality, bias, distribution shift, privacy, security, and governance challenges in machine learning.

Introduction

This assignment examines three practical challenges across the machine-learning lifecycle. It considers how data preparation, fairness, privacy, and security affect the reliability and responsible deployment of ML systems.

Objective

Analyze data and model risks in practical scenarios and identify approaches that can help improve data quality, reduce unfair outcomes, and protect sensitive information.

Process

  • Examined data-cleaning concerns and the risk of data leakage during model development.
  • Considered how historical bias and distribution differences can create unfair outcomes across groups.
  • Reviewed privacy risks in collaborative healthcare machine learning, including membership inference against a trained model.
  • Considered safeguards and governance approaches for more responsible model development and deployment.

Tools and technologies

The assignment discusses data validation and leakage prevention, subgroup fairness and distribution shift, federated learning, secure aggregation, and differential privacy. These are methods and technical concepts analyzed in the assignment; no specific software implementation is claimed.

Value proposition of the artifact

Unique value: It brings together data quality, fairness, and privacy risks instead of evaluating model performance alone. It also recognizes that protecting data during collaboration does not automatically eliminate privacy risks from a trained model.

Relevance: These considerations matter to AI/ML practitioners, security professionals, and organizations that develop or use models involving sensitive data. The work connects responsible AI principles with practical security and governance concerns.

References and supporting material

See the original assignment submission for the complete scenario analysis and any references included in the submitted document.
